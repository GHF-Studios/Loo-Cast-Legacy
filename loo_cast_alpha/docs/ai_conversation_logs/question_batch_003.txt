Capability Bedrock
1. Is a Capability primarily a runtime graph node, a contract surface, an API surface, an authority surface, or a single concept intentionally spanning all of these?
A: A single concept intentionally spanning all of these.
2. Should "Capability" be allowed to mean both a type/category of ability and one materialized runtime instance of that ability?
3. If we need two terms, should they be "Capability Type" and "Capability Instance", or is that old framing misleading?
A: Wellllllll, yesn't? Like, yes, a capability is supposed to be able to itself BE a type/category of (cap-)ability, BUT crucially, one capability cannot be it's own type, that's a circular dependency. But, non.circular "dependencies" are what actualla kinda form (part of) the capability graph, yk? I hope that framing helps.
4. Is a Capability always addressable by a stable identity/path, or can some capabilities be anonymous/internal implementation nodes?
A: Ah, good question. Hm. I suppose anonymous/internal implementation and usage may be permitted, but idk, this should be an exception only for internally orchestrating exceedingly large and complex modules/capabilities, yk? But even then, I don't think anonymity is a good idea.... no, don't allow anonymous capabilities, but private/internal as opposed to public is okay. We can even fully adher to rust's visibility modifier semantics with `in super` and `in crate` variants, yk?
5. Is every meaningful orchestration seam supposed to be represented as a Capability, or only seams that cross package/language/product boundaries?
A: I think my previous answer answers this as well and leans towards *a lot* of meaningful orchestration seams bing represented as a Capability, yk? Not EVERY little thing, but the larger structure and architecture or whatever, ofc, yk? It's kinda just not as blck-or-white as your question made this out to be, yk?
6. Can a Capability exist entirely inside Rust with no Rhai declaration surface?
A: Yes! Rhai ITSELF is a capability! Yk? Extremely pivotal question (and answer!), but yeah, there ain't much to say. I said what I said. But this SURELY needs further exploration.
7. Can a Capability exist entirely as a Rhai declaration with no custom Rust implementation beyond generic host support?
A: No, not really. For that rhai would need access to very fundamental and low-level functionality and data and all, and I am simply not willing to make rust expose every little bit as a capability, yk? Rhai can add and subtract integers or do other simple stuff on it's own, and it really shouldn't be doing anything else beside that stuff on it's own anyway. So, I suppose I coulda foregone all this babblign and just told you plain and simple that the answer is: No.
8. Is "Capability == ABI-ish protocol surface" a useful phrase, or does it overstate the ABI analogy?
A: Sounds about right.
9. Should capabilities be considered part of Vapor even when implemented by Spacetime, Loo Cast, or a third-party mod?
A: Well, yeah, in the sense that Vapor is one gigantic thing that defined what it even means for a Capability to be just that: A capability. Yk? I mean yeah we can totally rework an experience in roblox for example, while we still fundamentally have to use roblox primitives (generic primitives in our case, cause rust has superpowers over roblox with it's lua, pfffft, lol), yk? In that sense, cpabilities always need to like define metadata I suppose, metadata which is always in the same established format, the Vapor Specification for Content Creation, yk?
10. What is the smallest example of a real Capability that is not USF-specific?
A: Printing to the console, or maybe a biiiiit more generalized like printing output, or logging, idk like what abstraction level is useful, but yk what I mean? Or getting config values from a specific format of config values, like... a config system god damn it , lol. Or like "core" stuff, aka the whole app startup could be a capability in itself, yk? Capabilities as basically metadata-wrapped plain functions and data mostly, yk? Like, in rust we can easily represent an entire bevy App for example with ONE type, one single type, without any side effects needed to make use of that type. My vision is just to extend that flexibility by adding metadata so we can have a sort of abstract semantics tree so we can reason about who does what and why and with what authority or like..... idk I am drifting again, sorry!
